# Recheck: adaptive reassessment in the emergency waiting room

**Research brief · November 7, 2026 · Proposed adult workflow for clinical review**

Recheck should sit between initial triage and first clinician assessment. Its job is to surface meaningful changes, prompt an in-person check when patients cannot report reliably, and track whether someone assessed them. Recheck is, in effect, Martha's Rule for the waiting room, delivered on the patient's phone (§3a). Build one shared escalation workflow, a base check-in for everyone, and four follow-up groups chosen from the CEDIS complaint: chest/back pain, stroke-like or neurological change, head injury, and confused or drowsy. Use the base check-in alone for other complaints and add more groups after measuring local demand and staff workload.

This is a targeted evidence review, not a systematic review or a validated protocol. It draws on public Alberta policy and review documents, Canadian guidelines and administrative data, primary ED studies, and official UK programme documents. I did not find a public Foothills dataset linking initial complaint, staff reassessments, waiting-room deterioration, and confirmed diagnostic misses, or a public copy of Foothills' configured Epic triage screens.

## 1. What Alberta already requires

AHS HCS-181-01 §1 assigns initial CTAS to a trained clinician using the presenting complaint, clinical assessment and vital signs. The public guideline includes temperature, pulse, BP, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation and pain assessment including provoking factors, quality, radiation, severity and treatment. Glucose is checked when indicated. For altered consciousness, suspected stroke, seizure, suspected head injury or a fall with suspected head impact, staff assess consciousness, GCS, pupils and limb motor/sensory findings. The guideline gives clinical elements, not the precise wording of Foothills' intake questions. CTAS uses the standardized CEDIS presenting-complaint vocabulary. The national CTAS committee released revised guidelines in 2025, after the 2023 AHS document. Confirm which version and complaint mappings Foothills uses. Preserve the source code and vocabulary version, including when the local mapping is unknown. [AHS guideline §§1–2 and Appendix A](https://publicshare.albertahealthservices.ca/teams/policydocuments/1/clp-ed-assess-reassess-pts-guideline-hcs-181-01.pdf) · [2025 national CTAS guidelines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40938532/) · [2016 CTAS revisions and CEDIS background](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-emergency-medicine/article/revisions-to-the-canadian-emergency-department-triage-and-acuity-scale-ctas-guidelines-2016/E2CB3E2063C54E11259313FA4FEAE495)

| Initial CTAS | AHS Appendix A staff reassessment and documentation |
|---|---|
| 1 | Ongoing in an appropriate treatment area. |
| 2 | 15–60 minutes. Immediate treatment area, or continuous visual range of the triaging clinician if space is unavailable. |
| 3 | 30–120 minutes. |
| 4 | 60–120 minutes. |
| 5 | 120 minutes. |

Staff reassess sooner as indicated by clinical judgment or a change. Staff repeat and document a complete set of vitals for a marked change. A clinician can adjust care priority while the original CTAS remains on record. AHS also tells waiting patients to return immediately to triage if worsening and tell staff before leaving. **Keep separate deadlines for the patient prompt and staff reassessment.** The PRD's 15/30/60/120 schedule can be demo configuration. Confirm the actual site policy, current guideline version and each visit's staff reassessment plan before any live use. CTAS 1 is outside this workflow, and the QR does not provide the visual observation required for CTAS 2. [AHS guideline §§1–4, 8 and Appendix A](https://publicshare.albertahealthservices.ca/teams/policydocuments/1/clp-ed-assess-reassess-pts-guideline-hcs-181-01.pdf)

Acute Care Alberta's Grey Nuns review recommendations 5–7 address a physician-at-triage model, a designated physician lead when that role is unavailable, and an accountability framework. Recommendation 14 distinguishes screening for ischemic chest pain from considering other serious causes and asks reviewers to consider when bilateral BP monitoring is appropriate. Recommendation 15 explicitly proposes exploring digital tools that help staff identify who needs a more urgent assessment. The premier reported six EDs with triage physicians operating on November 7. Verify Foothills' current participation, staffing hours and escalation procedures with the site. Recheck is a proposed companion to the programme. The Grey Nuns review supports a synthetic scenario of evolving chest/back pain and a repeat staff measurement, with a documented handoff. It does not establish that a phone survey would have changed Sreekumar's outcome. [Acute Care Alberta public release](https://acutecarealberta.ca/Page48.aspx) · [Review recommendations](https://www.acutecarealberta.ca/aca-qa-review-2026-01-14.pdf) · [Alberta October 16 programme statement](https://www.alberta.ca/release.cfm?xID=97966C5AB1D6F-D276-21FB-8F856C5F7965EDF3) · [November 7 rollout reporting](https://globalnews.ca/news/12141285/albertas-hospital-triage-program-now-in-operation-danielle-smith/)

## 2. What gets missed and how to choose scope

| Evidence | What it can tell Recheck |
|---|---|
| AHRQ review: stroke, myocardial infarction, aortic aneurysm/dissection, spinal cord compression/injury and venous thromboembolism accounted for 39% of estimated serious misdiagnosis harms across the reviewed ED evidence. Traumatic brain injury/traumatic intracranial hemorrhage ranked ninth. Stroke misdiagnosis varied by presentation, with higher estimates for dizziness/vertigo than for weakness. [AHRQ review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK588123/) | Useful for choosing high-consequence signals and testing atypical presentations. The ranking spans the ED diagnostic episode. It is neither an initial-triage miss rate nor an Alberta prevalence estimate. Concussion and traumatic intracranial hemorrhage are distinct. |
| A 2025 US cohort found 2,175 of 5,929 patients with three selected time-sensitive diagnoses were undertriaged using ESI. Lower urgency was associated with CT delays in aortic dissection and subarachnoid hemorrhage. [Primary cohort](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2833434) | Evidence of a triage-specific gap in selected diagnosed cases. The denominator excludes other arrivals, ESI differs from CTAS, and the association does not establish the effect of Recheck. |
| CIHI's 2025–26 national reporting identified abdominal/pelvic pain and throat/chest pain among the most frequent **main problem diagnoses**. NACRS separately defines presenting complaint and discharge diagnosis fields. [CIHI volumes](https://www.cihi.ca/en/nacrs-emergency-department-visits-and-lengths-of-stay) · [NACRS metadata](https://www.cihi.ca/en/national-ambulatory-care-reporting-system-nacrs-metadata) | Chest and abdominal pain merit coverage review. Rank local presenting complaints using the appropriate field and local denominator. A discharge diagnosis cannot tell when deterioration was detectable. |
| An Ontario cohort associated longer shift-level ED stays with worse short-term outcomes after departure. A waiting-area wireless vital sign study established feasibility of capturing measurements in selected patients. [Ontario cohort](https://www.bmj.com/content/342/bmj.d2983) · [Waiting-area monitoring study](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000002322) | Supports studying the waiting interval and complementing patient reports with measured observations. A 14-ED Alberta cohort found no overall association between time to first physician assessment and seven-day mortality. The different measures and populations matter. None of these studies validates adaptive QR checks or proves they improve outcomes. [Alberta cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40715992/) |

For the first build, cover **chest/back pain**, **new neurological change regardless of presenting complaint**, **head injury**, and **confused or drowsy** presentations (CEDIS 401, 402, 405, 751, 854), where the patient's own report is least reliable. Use a universal route for patient/companion concern, new breathing difficulty, inability to participate and missed prompts. Do not offer the app to children, pregnancy over 20 weeks, self-harm or violence presentations, or major trauma; keep those visits on the staff list with the staff timer running. Evaluate dedicated abdominal/pelvic pain, breathing/infection and spine pathways next. They require their own evidence and clinical review, including local routing for presentations such as pregnancy-related pain and suspected sepsis. Keep paediatrics and obstetric-specific pathways separate. Choose expansion by local complaint volume, observed delays and harm, whether an observable change can be captured early, and whether staff can act on it.

To estimate the local gap, ask the site for a governed, de-identified encounter review. Count adult eligible waiting encounters by CEDIS group and CTAS, and record waiting duration, first staff reassessment, repeat vitals, patient/family concern, acuity or location changes, and clinician-adjudicated outcomes. Sample ordinary visits as well as returns, admissions, critical events and reports of missed deterioration. Have two clinicians independently classify whether a signal was present at triage, arose while waiting, appeared only later, or has insufficient documentation. Use eligible visits and waiting patient-hours as denominators. A mismatch between complaint and diagnosis can flag a chart for review. It does not itself prove undertriage or a diagnostic error.

## 3. UK precedents and what transfers

- **NHS Pathways:** The 111/999 clinical decision support system uses interlinked symptom questions. Answers determine the next question, with life-threatening concerns checked early and clear actions at endpoints. Clinicians govern updates and receive reports of safety issues. Adopt that architecture and change process. Its telephone dispositions and staffing model differ from an Alberta ED. [How Pathways works](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/nhs-pathways/how-it-works) · [Clinical governance](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/nhs-pathways/clinical-safety-and-governance)
- **Martha's Rule:** A structured wellness question and a route for patients, families and staff to request rapid review when concerns go unanswered. NHS England's phase 3 began extending the programme into ED, maternity and neonatal settings in 2026. Build a persistent concern button and agree who provides a second review. The published management data do not establish the effect of a QR waiting-room system. [Core standards](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/marthas-rule-core-standards/) · [July 2026 programme reporting](https://www.england.nhs.uk/statistics/statistical-work-areas/marthas-rule/statistical-work-areas-marthas-rule-marthas-rule-2026-27/statistical-work-areas-marthas-rule-statistical-work-areas-marthas-rule-programme-july2026/)
- **UK ED eTriage:** A study of 25,333 paired assessments in two EDs found 10.1% undertriage and 59.2% overtriage versus nurse triage among ambulatory adults. That comparator measures disagreement, not error against a perfect clinical reference. The tool addressed arrival, not serial reassessment. Use the result to motivate measuring both missed signals and alert workload. [Primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34545027/) · [NHS urgent-care self-service](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/urgent-care-self-service-product)
- **RCEM and NEWS2:** RCEM crowding guidance suggests assigning staff to check waiting areas and considering repeat initial assessment, including vitals, after prolonged waits. NEWS2 uses measured physiology and is a useful example of a staff observation and response system. Keep any such score under the site's existing protocol, separate from phone answers. [RCEM crowding guidance](https://rcem.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/RCEM_Crowding_Guidance_Jan_2024_final.pdf) · [RCP NEWS2](https://rcp.ac.uk/resources/national-early-warning-score-news-2/)

## 3a. Martha's Rule as the base

Martha's Rule has three parts, and each maps to a part of Recheck:

| Martha's Rule | Recheck |
|---|---|
| A structured wellness question, asked at least daily | The scheduled check-in, at CTAS-based intervals |
| Patients and families can call for an urgent review when worried | The always-visible "I'm worried" and "I need help now" buttons, for patient or companion |
| A rapid review by a clinician outside the treating team | Routing to the triage liaison physician or backup, with acknowledgement and assessment tracked |

The wellness question comes from the Patient Wellness Questionnaire: "How are you feeling?" and "How are you feeling compared with the last time you were asked?", each on a 5-point scale. Recheck adapts the second to "compared with when staff last checked on you". The supporting study was small (103 oncology inpatients, one hospital). Self-rated wellness predicted the next early-warning score after adjusting for baseline, and the authors call this preliminary. Ratings were recorded at only 14% of eligible observations. [Albutt et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8612917)

Programme data support the family route and show its load. From September 2024 to May 2026, 72% of escalation calls came through family or carers. About 31% concerned acute deterioration. For 222 calls that led to a transfer of care, the patient's early-warning score would not have triggered escalation. [NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/2026/03/over-10000-calls-to-marthas-rule-in-first-16-months-to-trigger-urgent-review-of-care/)

Most calls are about something other than deterioration, so give non-clinical questions their own route ("Question about my wait or care") that goes to the triage nurse, not the physician. A seven-trust A&E pilot (September 2025 to March 2026) logged 69 calls without extra clinical staff. NHS England plans to extend the rule to every A&E, including waiting rooms, by March 2028. [Nursing Times](https://www.nursingtimes.net/emergency-and-critical-care/marthas-rule-set-to-be-expanded-to-all-ae-departments-22-09-2026/)

## 4. What the adaptive check should do

Start with who is answering, whether there is an urgent concern, and how things have changed since the last assessment. Use triage complaint and observations to select a short follow-up branch. A new symptom can open a different branch regardless of initial complaint. Preserve answer options for baseline/unchanged, unsure, and unable to observe. A concerning finding immediately records and routes a task. Optional details may be collected afterward without blocking delivery. The urgent UI tells the person to get nearby staff attention immediately.

| Pathway | Patient or companion input | Staff action to route |
|---|---|---|
| **Chest/back pain** | Patient describes new or worsening pain, abrupt onset, change in location, or new spread. Capture fainting, breathing change or new neurological symptoms if reported. Compare to the documented baseline where available. | Review the change and repeat vitals per the local pathway. The staff workflow can display measured BP trends and existing tests with provenance. A pain description, BP value or negative ischemia test alone cannot clear other serious chest-pain causes. [Canadian acute aortic syndrome guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7828987/) · [Grey Nuns recommendations](https://www.acutecarealberta.ca/aca-qa-review-2026-01-14.pdf) |
| **Sudden neurological change** | A companion can report observed facial asymmetry, one-sided weakness, or a new speech/language change. Offer brief FAST-style observer guidance only if appropriate and only when it will not delay getting help. Also capture newly reported vision or balance changes for clinical review. Record onset or last known well separately if known. If the person is suddenly unable to communicate or there is no reliable observer for a concerning change, request bedside review. | Route immediately for clinical assessment under the site stroke pathway. A negative or incomplete FAST observation gives no clearance. In a retrospective stroke-centre study, about 14% of patients with confirmed acute ischemic stroke had no FAST symptoms recorded at presentation. This was a chart review of known strokes, not a test of companion screening in waiting rooms. Keep neurological exam, glucose and stroke activation in the staff workflow. [NHS FAST](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/) · [Aroor et al., stroke cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28082668/) · [Canadian Stroke Best Practices](https://www.strokebestpractices.ca/recommendations/acute-stroke-management/emergency-department-evaluation-and-management) |
| **Head injury** | Ask about change in headache and vomiting. A companion can observe increased drowsiness, confusion, unusual behaviour, or new difficulty speaking or moving. Capture witness information and timing where available. | Request an in-person neuro check for a concerning change. Staff record GCS components, pupils, limb findings and vitals using the site protocol. NICE's serial observation schedule is for admitted head-injury observation. Sport CRT6 is a lay recognition tool and SCAT6 is a clinician sport assessment. Neither supplies a validated waiting-room phone test. [NICE NG232 §§1.5 and 1.9](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng232/chapter/recommendations) · [CRT6 authors](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/11/689) · [AHS guideline §1.1](https://publicshare.albertahealthservices.ca/teams/policydocuments/1/clp-ed-assess-reassess-pts-guideline-hcs-181-01.pdf) |
| **No response** | Track no report received, partial response, inability reported by an observer, no device or assistance needed, and known delivery/connection failure separately. An expired browser session does not establish patient status. | Create one due-check task per outstanding episode per site policy. Require a documented outcome, such as located and assessed, moved, declined, or not located and handed off. Known technical failure also needs an operational route. Keep the task visible when there is no usable digital channel. |

Example prompts for clinician and patient review:

- **Patient, baseline:** “How are you feeling right now?” and “Compared with when staff last checked on you, how do you feel?”, each on a 5-point scale (§3a). Include “Not sure” and a separate “I need help now” action.
- **Patient, chest/back follow-up:** “Has the pain moved or spread somewhere new?” If yes, offer locations, then “When did that change start?” only if it does not hold up review.
- **Companion, new neurological change:** “Is their speech suddenly different from usual?” If yes, show the direct-to-staff instruction and route immediately. Then optionally ask “When were they last speaking and acting as usual?” Keep approximate and unknown times distinct.
- **Companion, head injury:** “Are they harder to wake or more confused than when staff last checked?” A concerning response ends the routine form and starts the staff observation workflow.
- **Staff, focused review:** Display the triggering observation and timing, then the locally approved exam fields or a link to the chart. Record who performed the assessment and when. Use a separate disposition for an unsuccessful attempt to locate the patient.

A dynamic *test* in the first release should mean a prompted observation by a companion or a focused exam assigned to staff. The one exception is a reliability check, not a clinical test: the patient counts circles shown one at a time and ignores squares. It is Recheck's own design, inspired by the DelApp counting task, which separated delirium from dementia and controls in small staff-administered inpatient studies. [Tieges et al.](https://core.ac.uk/download/288348629.pdf) It is unvalidated for self-use. A wrong count, or a prompt shown but not answered, only creates a non-urgent in-person staff check and never labels the patient. Timed taps, walking or balance challenges, phone-camera pupil measurements and automated speech judgments would need independent validation for this population and setting. They should be separate studies.

Companions are optional. The patient answers their own check-in when able, and a companion can join from the same visit or answer for them; pain and the circle check are asked only of the patient. In the neurological, head-injury and confused groups, a worrying patient answer counts fully. A reassuring one ("same", "better") never cancels a staff check, because patients may not notice their own change. In one cohort, 6.6% of TIA patients were unaware of their symptoms but had them witnessed by bystanders. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32961590/) With no companion present, the staff in-person schedule carries the safety net. Language, fatigue, pre-existing deficits, device access and the inability to respond can all change what information is available. Preserve baseline limitations and the observer's role and language. Design for someone who cannot hold or operate a phone.

## 4a. Telling patients about triage

No public AHS source says whether Calgary triage staff tell patients their CTAS level. [HCS-181-01](https://publicshare.albertahealthservices.ca/teams/policydocuments/1/clp-ed-assess-reassess-pts-guideline-hcs-181-01.pdf) requires staff to tell every waiting patient to return to triage if they get worse and to tell staff before leaving. It says nothing about disclosing the level. The AHS [wait times FAQ](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/about/data/ahs-data-edm-understanding-wait-times-patients.pdf) explains that "the sickest patients will always be seen first" and that the ED "is not first come first served", without mentioning a patient's own level. [Nova Scotia Health](https://www.nshealth.ca/sites/default/files/documents/Understanding%20Triage%20poster.pdf) tells patients openly that they get a score from 1 to 5, with examples for each level. In a [Melbourne observational study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13431858/), triage nurses sometimes gave the category and its target time, while about a third of Australian patients wait longer than that target. Telling patients about expected delays at triage raised satisfaction in a [pre-post study](https://pxjournal.org/journal/vol2/iss2/6/) of about 1,200 patients. I found no study of disclosing the level itself. Calgary practice is probably informal and varies by nurse.

We had no triage nurse to ask, so the demo assumes the following:

- **Every patient sees a "How triage works" card.** It repeats AHS's public message: the sickest are seen first, answers never change your place in line, return to triage if you get worse, and tell staff before you leave.
- **Showing a patient's own level in plain words is a site setting** that the triage nurse can switch on or off. It is off by default. The wording describes the level assigned at triage, because a clinician can later change priority in Connect Care while the initial CTAS stays on record (§1).
- **Never show CTAS target times** (the `ctas_interval()` values). Real waits often run several hours, so targets would contradict the department estimate and lose trust.
- **Confirm before a pilot.** Ask triage nurses and patient advisers whether staff tell patients their level, and review the wording with them.

## 5. Deterministic rules and data model

The prototype uses Python/Pydantic for its contract, FastAPI for the planned service, PostgreSQL with Tiger Data/TimescaleDB, and React for the planned UI. Keep **question selection**, **clinical signal detection**, and **operational routing** separate. Version question banks and rules and link clinical releases to evidence, approval, population, site and effective dates. Use a bounded condition format with allowlisted fields and operators.

### Current contract and persistence

The [Pydantic contract](../../api/README.md) and [database schema](../../db/schema.sql) define the prototype model. The API in [`api/`](../../api/README.md) implements the service, the rules, the question bank, the timers and the live dashboard updates.

| Records | Current design |
|---|---|
| `visit`, `visit_session` | Site, initial CTAS 2–5, complaint group, optional CEDIS code, language, channel, visit status and separate patient and staff due fields. One QR credential per visit, active while the visit is waiting; whoever scans it chooses patient or companion, and only one device is signed in at a time (a second device must confirm to take over). Store a token hash for lookup and a server-encrypted copy so the desk can show the same QR again; the planned API exchanges the token for an HttpOnly session cookie and enforces revocation. Each phone report records its session, and the database checks the reporter matches the role chosen at sign-in. The schema carries no patient name, health number or EHR encounter ID. |
| `submission`, `report` | Client submission UUID and fingerprint support retry deduplication. Reports retain capture and receipt times, distinguish reporter from channel, and cover check-ins, concerns, findings, intent to leave and staff flags. A check-in pins the question bank version. An answer has a value or a coded reason for being missing. Pain is self-reported; companion pain must be missing. |
| `vitals` | Staff-entered measurements with measured and received times, staff identity and a reference to the original record when correcting it. |
| `task`, `task_reason` | One active task per visit, with multiple routing roles in one row and versioned reasons linked to evidence. Severity can rise, recipients can be added, and an existing acknowledgement deadline cannot move later. First delivery, acknowledgement and completed assessment have distinct fields. Completion requires acknowledgement and a disposition. |
| `audit_log` | Append-only audit trail. Write transactions require an explicit actor. Audit payloads omit credential hashes. Reports, vitals, and audit records are TimescaleDB hypertables; submission IDs use a separate regular table for uniqueness across time partitions. |

The schema initializes both visit deadlines from demo CTAS defaults. The runtime must enforce independent advancement of those clocks. Separate prompt delivery attempts, per-recipient delivery tracking, assessment deadlines, named ownership transfers and linked EHR encounter IDs are potential extensions. The current task has one acknowledgement deadline, one first-delivery timestamp and routing by role. The handover response lists open work; it does not implement ownership transfer. The dashboard event contract carries change notices and leaves transport open.

Illustrative decision table for synthetic cases. The local clinical team sets real triggers and deadlines.

| Event and condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Prompt starts; chest/back complaint with no urgent signal entered | Offer the chest/back follow-up items. |
| Any complaint; observer reports new facial droop or speech difficulty | Create urgent review immediately and display the direct-to-staff instruction. Offer last-known-well as optional after routing. |
| Head injury; companion reports increasing drowsiness | Create urgent staff assessment task. A conflicting “same” from the patient remains visible and cannot cancel the task. |
| Check-in deadline; no usable report, still waiting | Open or update a missed-check task. Independently evaluate the staff reassessment deadline. |
| Urgent task passes its acknowledgement deadline | Add the configured backup role and log the escalation. Server receipt, dashboard delivery, acknowledgement and assessment remain distinct. Delivery and assessment deadlines beyond acknowledgement are pilot design work. |
| Shift ends or visit moves | Surface unresolved tasks and their routing roles in handover. Confirm named transfer before removing a task from a prior owner's view once that workflow is implemented. |

An illustrative rule format for synthetic replay. The example is design pseudocode, separate from the checked-in Pydantic report and receipt models. A finding report already denotes a new finding, and the service should map rule outputs to the contract. The first rule changes the form. The second fires even if the original complaint is unrelated and the form is still incomplete.

```json
[
  {
    "id": "show-chest-change-v1",
    "on": "prompt_started",
    "when": {"field": "visit.complaint_group", "eq": "chest_back_pain"},
    "then": {"show_items": ["pain_change", "pain_location_change"]}
  },
  {
    "id": "new-speech-change-v1",
    "on": "finding_submitted",
    "when": {"all": [
      {"field": "finding.code", "eq": "speech_change"},
      {"field": "finding.change_from_baseline", "eq": "new"}
    ]},
    "then": {
      "route": "urgent_staff_review",
      "show_instruction": "get_nearby_staff_now",
      "offer_after_routing": ["last_known_well"]
    }
  }
]
```

An unknown baseline should stay unknown and have its own disposition when staff review is needed. Evaluate all matching urgent rules before selecting more questions. Attach reasons to a single active task and preserve the earliest actionable deadline. Review clinical content and translations as a versioned package. Replay historical fixtures before publication, pin the version on every decision and keep a rollback path.

Apply urgent rules to each explicit submitted finding, including incomplete forms. Persist report intake, rule reasons and task changes in one transaction. Choose and implement recoverable delivery separately; an outbox is an option if durable delivery is required. Use submission IDs and a unique active-task key to deduplicate retries while retaining distinct reports. Run deadline checks on the server and reconcile after outages. Recheck encounter state before routing a late offline submission and retain both timestamps. State tests should cover duplicate reports, positive signal followed by a negative answer, missing data, disconnected devices, simultaneous staff actions, disconnected dashboards, no assigned clinician, and handoff. Measure both acknowledgement and bedside assessment. Staff closure needs a disposition and evidence or a reference to the clinical record.

## 6. Pilot and Connect Care integration

**Synthetic demo.** Run an evolving chest-pain case, a companion reporting a neurological change during an unrelated visit, and an unanswered head-injury check. Show which question changed, why the task fired, what happens if nobody acknowledges, and the encounter's full timeline. Mark prototype thresholds as demonstration values.

**Before real patient use.** Meet with triage nurses, charge nurses, liaison/backup physicians, patient advisers, Connect Care, and privacy/safety leads. Ask for the current site reassessment grid, triage screen fields, chest pain nurse-initiated protocol, stroke activation, head-injury observation, pager or physical escalation, and downtime and handoff procedures. Replay cases with clinicians. Then use a governed shadow evaluation with routine care and escalation intact, followed by a staffed pilot only after acceptance criteria and fallback procedures are agreed. Compare rule findings with blinded clinical review of escalated and sampled non-escalated visits to avoid measuring only alerts that fired. Report missed clinical deterioration, time from signal to acknowledgement and assessment, reassessments completed on schedule, tasks per 100 waiting patient-hours, missed-check outcomes, duplicate alerts, staff workload, and access/completion by language, channel and assistance needs. Treat rare serious outcomes as exploratory in a small pilot.

**Integration.** Use the Connect Care encounter as the clinical source of truth, with the approved clinical record holding staff assessment and priority. Start with a synthetic adapter. Ask Connect Care which encounter/complaint, vitals, care-phase and staff-worklist integrations are approved at the site. Connect Care already uses attending handover and staffed pools in its ED results workflow, a useful precedent for assigning and transferring ownership. Its late-results inbox is a different workflow from urgent waiting-room escalation. Epic publishes FHIR capabilities, but availability in its catalog does not establish Foothills permissions or a Rover inbox endpoint. HL7 Questionnaire/QuestionnaireResponse is a useful model for stable items and conditional display. Keep the versioned escalation rules and task state independent of the UI schema. Get the custodian's assessment of privacy impact and intended-use regulatory review before implementing any real-data workflow.

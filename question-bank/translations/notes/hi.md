# Hindi (hi) translation notes for reviewer

1. **Gender in first-person buttons.** Hindi verbs must show gender, so first-person buttons use slash forms: "मैं जा रहा/रही हूँ", "मैं स्टाफ़ को बता रहा/रही हूँ", "मैं यह नहीं कर सकता/सकती", "नहीं, मैं रुक रहा/रही हूँ". Please check that these read clearly on small buttons.
2. **Second and third person.** Where we could, we used constructions with no gender: the ergative ("आपने … इंतज़ार किया है"), "आपकी तबीयत/उनकी हालत कैसी है", and the subjunctive ("कल्पना कर सकें", "अगर आप बीच में छोड़ें"). Where that wasn't possible we used the honorific plural ("क्या आप जा रहे हैं?", "आप चेक-इन में मदद कर रहे हैं", "मरीज़ जा रहे हैं", "ट्राइएज नर्स … आएँगे", "डॉक्टर या नर्स … आएँगे"). That plural is grammatically masculine, so check that it reads as neutral and polite.
3. **Clinician** is translated as "डॉक्टर या नर्स" (doctor or nurse). There is no everyday Hindi word for "clinician". Confirm that this fits a site where a nurse practitioner may do the assessment.
4. **Seizure (fit)**: "दौरा (मिर्गी जैसा दौरा, seizure)". The bare word दौरा can also mean "attack" in general, so "like an epileptic fit" was added. Check that this doesn't suggest the patient has epilepsy.
5. **Confusion/drowsiness**: we used "उलझन (कन्फ़्यूज़न)" for confused/muddled. उलझन can also suggest worry or perplexity, which is why the English loanword is in brackets. "Harder to stay awake" is "जागते रहना मुश्किल", and the companion form is "जगाना या जगाए रखना".
6. **Last known well**: "moving as usual" is translated as "हिलना-डुलना" (moving the body and limbs), not "चलना" (walking), to match the stroke-onset meaning.
7. **Body locations**: "lower back" is "कमर (पीठ का निचला हिस्सा)". कमर can also mean the waist. "Between the shoulder blades" is "पीठ में, दोनों कंधों के बीच".
8. **"Last check"** (headache_change) is translated generically as "पिछली बार" because the English could mean the last check-in or the last staff check.
9. **"I'm not sure"** is "मुझे पक्का नहीं पता" ("I don't know for sure"). It is meant to read as unknown, not as a no.
10. **Triage** is kept as "ट्राइएज (triage)" because Canadian ED signage uses the English word. **Health number** became "हेल्थ कार्ड नंबर" to match Alberta usage. **Synthetic data** is "नमूना (सिंथेटिक) डेटा".
11. **Option labels** (feeling, symptoms) use feminine agreement to match तबीयत/तकलीफ़ें. The headache options use masculine agreement to match सिरदर्द. Check that they still make sense if the UI shows them alone.
- **Triage card (`triageInfo`):** Level names: "बहुत ज़रूरी / ज़रूरी / कम ज़रूरी / ज़रूरी नहीं". The nurse sentences use "द्वारा" (passive) to avoid gender. Check that "ज़रूरी नहीं" doesn't read as "care not needed". No target times are shown on purpose; please keep it that way.
